# Layer 9 — Threat Prioritization

## Purpose and Scope

Layer 9 introduces the **threat-prioritization engine** for the ADITI Space Situational Awareness platform.

The primary objective is to identify catalogued space objects that may pose a threat to protected active satellites, screen for close approaches, and prioritize events using an explainable multi-factor heuristic risk-priority index.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Scientific & Operational Disclaimer:**
> The risk-priority score generated by Layer 9 is a **heuristic priority index** designed to assist operators in triaging attention. It is **NOT** a collision probability ($P_c$) and must not be treated as an operational conjunction assessment or maneuver-decision metric. Computing validated collision probability requires full state error covariance matrices and object cross-sectional dimensions, which are not present in general perturbations (GP) data.

## Data-Source Design & Explicit Limitations

### 1. Available Dataset
- The platform's active catalog source is `data/raw/active_satellites_gp.csv` (16,047 active satellites).
- It provides mean orbital elements in OMM/GP format necessary for SGP4 propagation.

### 2. Lack of SATCAT Categorization
- General Perturbations (GP) records do not include SATCAT categorization fields such as `OBJECT_TYPE` (Payload, Rocket Body, Debris), `RCS_SIZE`, or launch country metadata.
- Consequently, without a separate GP source, screening operates in **active-vs-active** mode.

### 3. Extensibility for Debris & Inactive Objects
- The current primary dataset contains active satellites only. Screening inactive satellites, rocket bodies, and debris requires suitable orbital data for those objects.
- `ThreatConfig.threat_gp_csv_path` allows configuring a separate GP dataset for debris or inactive objects.
- When this path is not configured or missing, the screening engine **explicitly documents this limitation** in `ScreeningResult.data_source_notes` and event metadata. The system does not silently restrict screening without reporting it.

## Risk-Priority Heuristic Formulation

The composite risk-priority score $R \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is computed as:

$$R = w_{\text{sep}} f_{\text{sep}} + w_{\text{vel}} f_{\text{vel}} + w_{\text{tca}} f_{\text{tca}} + w_{\text{anom}} f_{\text{anom}}$$

where weights are strictly constrained such that $\sum w_i = 1.0$ and $w_i \ge 0$.

### Factor Definitions & Rationale

| Factor | Notation | Default Weight | Formula | Operational / Physical Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Separation Distance** | $f_{\text{sep}}$ | 0.40 | $1 - \min\left(\frac{d_{\text{min}}}{d_{\text{ref}}}, 1\right)$ | **Geometric Proximity:** Smaller predicted miss distance at TCA indicates closer physical passage, the fundamental prerequisite for encounter risk. |
| **Relative Velocity** | $f_{\text{vel}}$ | 0.25 | $\min\left(\frac{v_{\text{rel}}}{v_{\text{ref}}}, 1\right)$ | **Encounter Severity / Consequences:** Higher relative speed implies exponentially greater kinetic energy ($E_k \propto v^2$) in the event of impact, yielding catastrophic fragmentation. |
| **Time to TCA** | $f_{\text{tca}}$ | 0.15 | $1 - \min\left(\frac{\Delta t_{\text{TCA}}}{\Delta t_{\text{ref}}}, 1\right)$ | **Urgency:** Approaches occurring sooner require faster tracking acquisition and operator attention. |
| **Anomaly Score** | $f_{\text{anom}}$ | 0.20 | Clamped $[0, 1]$ or $0.0$ | **Behavioural Context (Layer 8):** Elevates priority if the threat object exhibits anomalous orbital dynamics (e.g., unexpected drift or maneuver). |

### Neutral / Missing-Data Policy for Anomaly Scores
- Anomaly scoring is applied only if a fitted Layer 8 model exists and the threat object has valid historical feature rows in the Layer 7 feature store.
- **Temporal Alignment Constraint**: Unavailable or historically misaligned Layer 8 anomaly features cannot be presented as verified anomaly scores at the time of closest approach. Historical observations reflect past behaviour and must not be misrepresented as contemporaneous anomaly states at predicted TCA.
- If anomaly context is unavailable, $f_{\text{anom}} = 0.0$. Missing data is never penalized or arbitrarily imputed, and the absence is explicitly flagged in `risk_explanation` and `screening_notes`.

---

## Severity Classification

Scores are categorized into three discrete actionability tiers:
- **`critical`**: $R \ge 0.80$ (Requires immediate review)
- **`warning`**: $0.50 \le R < 0.80$ (Requires continued observation)
- **`low`**: $R < 0.50$ (Standard situational awareness)

## Computational Feasibility & Scaling

- Full pairwise conjunction screening scales as $\mathcal{O}(N \times M)$ where $N$ is the number of protected assets and $M$ is the size of the catalog.
- For a full catalog of 16,000 objects, all-to-all screening entails $\sim 128 \times 10^6$ pairwise SGP4 propagations over the screening window.
- `ThreatConfig.max_threat_candidates` bounds candidate evaluation per protected asset for single-machine environments.
